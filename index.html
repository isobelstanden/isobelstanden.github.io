About

Isobel Standen is a PhD student in philosophy at the University of York and a researcher at the Centre for Assuring Autonomy. Her research explores the contrast between human capacities and those attributed to AI systems. In particular, she highlights how it is a lack of reliable common sense that is one of the leading causes of errors made by AI systems. Her work is at the intersection of philosophy and computer science, where she collaborates with roboticists, software engineers, and domain experts to tackle the challenges of assuring ethical AI solutions.

Isobel is also a co-organiser of the annual ‘Requirement Engineering for Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence’ (‘RETRAI’) workshop which aims to bring together multidisciplinary researchers and practitioners to discuss the challenges associated with developing truly trustworthy AI systems and identify strategies for overcoming associated risks. Her PhD project is part of the UKRI Trustworthy Autonomous Systems node in resilience, supervised by Professor Alan Thomas (Department of Philosophy) and Professor Radu Calinescu (Department of Computer Science).
